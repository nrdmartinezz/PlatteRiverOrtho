As parents, nothing matters more than setting our kids up for success — and that includes nurturing a healthy, confident smile from the very start. But with so many questions around when to begin brushing, how to handle flossing, or when to seek professional care, it can feel like a lot to keep track of. At Platte River Orthodontics, Dr. Chad Carter and our team are here to guide you through every milestone of your child’s dental development with expert advice tailored for each stage of growth.

## From Birth to First Tooth: Gentle Beginnings (0–6 Months)

Good oral habits begin before a single tooth appears. After each feeding, gently clean your baby’s gums using a soft, damp cloth or infant gum brush to clear away milk residue and bacteria. This not only keeps their mouth fresh but also helps your little one get used to the sensation of oral care — a great foundation for the brushing routine ahead.

## Welcoming the First Tooth & First Dental Check-Up (6–12 Months)

When that first tiny tooth breaks through, it’s time for your child’s initial dental appointment. The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends seeing a dentist by age one or within six months of the first tooth eruption. At Platte River Orthodontics, our friendly, low-stress environment helps your child feel comfortable while Dr. Carter performs a gentle exam, checks for early signs of cavities, and offers personalized tips for daily care.

Start brushing with a soft infant toothbrush and just a smear of fluoride toothpaste — about the size of a grain of rice. Fluoride at this stage is crucial for strengthening enamel and warding off early decay.

## Toddlers & Early Oral Habits (1–3 Years)

These formative years are all about building routines. Keep brushing twice daily for your child, and introduce flossing once teeth start touching — usually by age two or three. Since toddlers aren’t yet skilled at brushing or flossing independently, parents should continue to handle this important task.

Pay close attention to diet: avoid putting your child to bed with bottles filled with milk, formula, or juice, as this can lead to “baby bottle tooth decay.” Instead, offer water between meals and limit juice intake to under 4 ounces daily, per pediatric guidelines.

## Preschool to Early Elementary: Encouraging Independence (3–6 Years)

As your child grows, encourage them to take part in their oral care while you supervise to ensure thorough brushing. At this stage, a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste is suitable. Making brushing fun — like playing a favorite two-minute song or using a timer — can help keep them engaged and consistent.

If your child’s first permanent molars start appearing (sometimes as early as age five or six), it’s a good time to discuss dental sealants with Dr. Carter. Sealants are a proven way to protect these new teeth from cavities by creating a barrier against plaque and food debris.

## School-Age Smiles: Protection & Transition (6–12 Years)

This is a dynamic period where baby teeth give way to permanent ones. Diligent brushing twice daily and flossing every day remain essential. Regular dental exams and cleanings every six months allow us to monitor tooth eruption, apply sealants to molars, and catch any alignment concerns early.

For active kids involved in sports, a custom-fitted mouthguard from Platte River Orthodontics offers superior protection against dental injuries compared to generic options. Safeguarding your child’s smile during play is a smart investment in their future.
