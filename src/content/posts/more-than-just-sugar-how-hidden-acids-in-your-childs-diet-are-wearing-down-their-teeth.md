When parents think about what harms their child’s teeth, sugar usually takes the spotlight. But at **Platte River Orthodontics**, Dr. Chad Carter wants families in Gretna, NE, to know there’s another sneaky enemy damaging young smiles: **dietary acids**. These acids quietly wear away enamel over time, leaving teeth vulnerable long before cavities become obvious.

Understanding how acid erosion differs from cavities — and spotting the acidic culprits lurking in everyday foods and drinks — can make a big impact on maintaining your child’s healthy, confident smile.

## Cavities and Acid Erosion: What Sets Them Apart?

Cavities are caused by bacteria feeding on sugars and producing acids that create tiny holes in the enamel. This process is bacterial and localized—preventable with good brushing habits and less sugar.

Acid erosion works differently. It’s a chemical reaction where acids directly break down enamel minerals. This happens through repeated exposure to acidic foods and beverages, thinning the enamel layer and making teeth more sensitive, dull, and prone to stains and decay. Unlike cavities, acid erosion isn’t caused by bacteria—it’s a direct enamel attack.

Notice how sports drinks and lemonade—often mistaken as healthier than soda—are actually among the worst offenders for enamel erosion. Even sparkling water, a trendy alternative, carries enough acid to cause wear if consumed too often.

## How to Safeguard Your Child’s Enamel

The great news? You can protect your child’s teeth from acid erosion with some simple habits:

- Make water the go-to drink. It’s the best way to keep teeth safe and rinse away acids.

- Use a straw with acidic beverages. This reduces contact between acid and teeth.

- Don’t brush right after acidic drinks or snacks. Enamel is softened and more vulnerable to abrasion. Rinse with water and wait about 30 minutes before brushing.
