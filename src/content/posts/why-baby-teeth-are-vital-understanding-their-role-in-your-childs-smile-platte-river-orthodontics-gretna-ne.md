“It’s just baby teeth—they’ll fall out anyway.” If you’ve ever heard this common phrase, you’re not alone. At **Platte River Orthodontics**, Dr. Chad Carter often hears parents underestimate the importance of primary teeth. But those little pearly whites do so much more than just pave the way for adult teeth—they’re essential for your child’s overall development, health, and future smile.

## The Crucial Roles Baby Teeth Play

From the moment the first tooth pops up around six months, baby teeth become hard workers in your child’s mouth for several years. Here’s how they contribute:

**Eating and Nutrition:** Baby teeth allow children to bite, chew, and enjoy a variety of foods—essential for getting the vitamins and minerals needed for healthy growth and brain development. When baby teeth suffer from decay or are lost too soon, kids may avoid certain foods, affecting their nutrition and well-being.

**Speech Skills:** Teeth are key partners in speech formation. Sounds like “t,” “d,” “s,” and “f” rely on the precise placement of the tongue against the teeth. Losing front teeth early can make it harder for kids to pronounce words clearly, sometimes requiring speech therapy down the line.

**Jaw and Facial Growth:** Baby teeth don’t just sit there—they stimulate the jawbone to grow properly. The roots keep the jawbone healthy and ensure there’s enough room for permanent teeth to come in straight and strong.

## Space Keepers: Protecting Your Child’s Future Smile

One of the most important jobs baby teeth do is acting as **natural placeholders**. Each primary tooth reserves space for its adult successor. If a baby tooth is lost too early—due to injury or decay—neighboring teeth can shift into the open spot. This crowding can cause misalignment that might require braces or other orthodontic treatments later.

To prevent this, Dr. Carter may suggest a **space maintainer**, a small device that holds the gap open until the permanent tooth is ready. This simple step can save your child from more extensive treatment down the road.
